# Targeted analytic-source intake

Checked 2026-10-31. These are other authors' published statements, not results
proved or independently verified in this repository.

- bramble_trace, [Paper 52, version 5](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/52/v5),
  Theorem 3.11, states a mixed ordinary/inverse energy bound
  `P+P^(1/3)*Y` for a principal fixed twist, with natural rows, common deletions,
  original separate profiles and repeated primes. Substituting the two contact
  lengths from our model gives exponent 1/3+r+n=1.5138591346..., exceeding the
  required 1.1186541994. Its general statement does not close this contract.
  Its Theorem 5.7 density exponent `(8-7*sigma)/(3*(2-sigma))`, at
  `sigma=(1+delta*)/2`, is about 0.779282, also above our selected-row count
  0.626569. A joint selected-row extension would require a separate argument.
  In addition, the current return uses sextic row characters. These cubic-family
  estimates need a family reduction before any comparison becomes an application.
- bramble_trace, [Paper 54, version 2](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/54/v2),
  states a strict saving for a prescribed signed Kubota remainder near an upper
  label range. It controls an output projection and leaves an inverse-character
  signal and complementary output. No identification with our full physical
  return or arbitrary source polynomials has been established.
- bramble_trace, [Paper 58, version 3](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/58/v3),
  gives a coherent source saving under an actual short-inverse-factor condition.
  Its pole-subtracted mean and retained coefficient restrictions require a
  source-to-consumer map. Its abstract explicitly leaves the full signed pole
  and complementary inverse configurations unresolved.
- bramble_trace, [Paper 10](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/10),
  treats sextic Kubota sources. It distinguishes the sharp generic norm from
  the estimate still needed for a specified Möbius/plain coefficient. Its
  general norm result supplies no improvement for that specific coefficient.

The arithmetic opportunity retained here is the support of one long squarefree
inverse position; see [inverse-dominant-support.md](inverse-dominant-support.md).
No novelty claim for mixed moments in general is intended.

A fixed vanishing Mellin moment is not sufficient to remove a polar source
uniformly at independent norm heights: twisting the profile translates the
Mellin argument along a vertical line. An identically vanishing transform on
that line would force the fixed profile to vanish. A height-dependent
annihilator would require a new reconstruction, including the removed polar
component. This observation only closes the simple fixed-profile shortcut.
