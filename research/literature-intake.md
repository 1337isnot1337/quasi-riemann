# Targeted analytic-source intake

Checked 2026-10-31. These are other authors' published statements, not results
proved or independently verified in this repository.

- bramble_trace, [Paper 52, version 5](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/52/v5),
  Theorem 3.11, states a mixed ordinary/inverse energy bound
  `P+P^(1/3)*Y` for a principal fixed twist, with natural rows, common deletions,
  original separate profiles and repeated primes. Substituting the two contact
  lengths from our model gives exponent 1/3+r+n=1.5138591346..., exceeding the
  required 1.1186541994. Its general statement does not close this contract.
  Its Theorem 5.7 density exponent `(8-7*sigma)/(3*(2-sigma))`, at
  `sigma=(1+delta*)/2`, is about 0.779282, also above our selected-row count
  0.626569. A joint selected-row extension would require a separate argument.
  In addition, the current return uses sextic row characters. These cubic-family
  estimates need a family reduction before any comparison becomes an application.
- bramble_trace, [Paper 54, version 2](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/54/v2),
  states a strict saving for a prescribed signed Kubota remainder near an upper
  label range. It controls an output projection and leaves an inverse-character
  signal and complementary output. No identification with our full physical
  return or arbitrary source polynomials has been established.
- bramble_trace, [Paper 58, version 3](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/58/v3),
  gives a coherent source saving under an actual short-inverse-factor condition.
  Its pole-subtracted mean and retained coefficient restrictions require a
  source-to-consumer map. Its abstract explicitly leaves the full signed pole
  and complementary inverse configurations unresolved.
  A direct assignment H=U, K=U^(r+n) at our contact violates its H>=K
  requirement since r+n=1.1805258.... Its one-long-inverse corollary instead
  requires H>=M^2 Q, which would require row exponent 2r+n=1.9333828....
  These substitutions are scope tests, not a claimed identification of the
  Kubota source with our physical numerator. A transformed norm could have
  different lengths and must be derived explicitly.
- bramble_trace, [Paper 75, version 2](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/75/v2)
  and [Paper 76, version 1](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/76/v1),
  retain actual Möbius signs, full coprimality and linked quadratic marks in
  sextic **function-field** means. Their character is the right order, but
  their mark is the quadratic cube of that character, not our ordinary
  numerator. Their geometric bounds assert no number-field transfer.
  Even granting a transfer and harmless restoration of inverse-column gcds,
  Paper 76's unmarked leading field-size exponent would give the positive
  energy model max(1, 1/2+2r/3), before its degree-constant cost. For
  1<=r<=3/2 its excess over the inherited (1+5r)/6 is exactly (2-r)/6>=1/12.
  Thus the stated gap alone is insufficient for the long inverse branch.
- bramble_trace, [Paper 5, version 3](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/5/v3),
  Theorem 1, gives the specified cubic Gauss mean in angular modes j=2,-2.
  The radial shared-label probe has mode j=0 and does not meet that contract.
  The [angular-order candidate](angular-cubic-probe.md) matches j=2, at the cost
  of replacing the four ordinary frequency poles by a single pole. Its full
  signal and frequency sum have not been established. The paper's stronger
  sextic mean concerns a different prescribed coefficient and character family.
- bramble_trace, [Paper 8, version 1](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/8/v1),
  Theorem 1, controls a fixed primitive sextic residue Type-I pairing with an
  unrestricted plain factor. Combining its displayed main term with inherited
  7/8 nonvanishing gives the [specific pole-pairing calculation](primitive-pole-pairing.md).
  It does not control the continuous contour or the complementary row space.
- bramble_trace, [Paper 10](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/10),
  treats sextic Kubota sources. It distinguishes the sharp generic norm from
  the estimate still needed for a specified Möbius/plain coefficient. Its
  general norm result supplies no improvement for that specific coefficient.
- Yuanda Xu and Hejian Sang, [arXiv:2610.17675v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17675v2),
  revised 2026-10-14, proves the matching full-range lower bound with both
  indices squarefree. Thus restricting a generic source to squarefree columns
  alone does not evade its sharp norm. This strengthens the older v1 comparison
  cited in Paper 10. The v2 structured prime moment has normalized balanced
  exponent `(31-10*sqrt(6))/6 = 1.0841837620...`, but its coefficients and
  balanced lengths differ from our inverse/plain block of total support
  U^1.1805258013. Its unmarked bin-count mechanism is already the p=0 branch
  of the inherited density model; a selected-row application needs extra work.

The arithmetic opportunity retained here is the support of one long squarefree
inverse position; see [inverse-dominant-support.md](inverse-dominant-support.md).
No novelty claim for mixed moments in general is intended.

A fixed vanishing Mellin moment is not sufficient to remove a polar source
uniformly at independent norm heights: twisting the profile translates the
Mellin argument along a vertical line. An identically vanishing transform on
that line would force the fixed profile to vanish. A height-dependent
annihilator would require a new reconstruction, including the removed polar
component. This observation only closes the simple fixed-profile shortcut.

The [coupled Mellin derivative](coupled-mellin-annihilator.md) is a different
candidate: it acts on independent physical scales and preserves the formal
signal. Its low estimate and coherent-row integration remain unproved.
